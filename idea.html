Your benchmark idea, taken seriously

The non-ergodic shopping agent

A user-side agent that must get everything, on time, within budget — where time and money are spent and gone. This is the exact axis τ-bench and SABER never touch: irreversibility. Here is why it's a real gap, how I'd design it so it stays verifiable, and where it could go wrong.

verdict: strong, and on-thesis for the lab irreversible / consumable state watch: don't collapse to a solver wrapper

Why this is genuinely new (not just τ-bench retail again)

τ-bench retail looks superficially similar — orders, prices, a shopping domain. But every structural choice is the opposite of what you're proposing. That opposition is the contribution.

The closest prior art is WebShop (single-attribute "find a matching product") and τ-retail. Neither has a hard budget + deadline that interact, and neither has irreversible commitment. To my knowledge that combination — solver-verifiable sourcing under a conjunctive deadline and a shared budget — is open.

The deep reason this is hard: the subgoals are coupled

"Biryani at 6 PM" is not n independent errands. The ingredients are tied together by two shared constraints, and that coupling is the whole game:

AND-deadline (makespan)

One late item fails everything

You need all ingredients by 6 PM. Success time = max over items, not sum or average. A single slow delivery — even a low-probability one — blows the whole task. The subgoals are conjunctive.

Shared budget

Buying A cheaply can foreclose B

Every dollar spent on one item (and each separate delivery fee, and the tip) is a dollar unavailable for the rest. A greedy "cheapest milk" choice can make the fast saffron vendor unaffordable. The items compete for one resource.

This is exactly the lab's subgoal-interaction lens. In τ-bench the subgoals are INDEPENDENT and reversible. Here they are coupled through a shared, non-replenishable resource and a shared makespan — the NON_SERIALIZABLE regime, made physical by irreversibility. Your benchmark is the planning-under-resources instantiation of the serializability program.

What "non-ergodic" really buys you (and why it's the right word)

Ergodic = the time-average equals the ensemble-average; you can learn by averaging over restarts. Your setting has absorbing failure states — bankruptcy (over budget) or a missed deadline are permanent for that episode. You cannot average your way out of ruin.

avoid ruin
The objective is not "minimize expected cost." It's "don't enter an absorbing-failure state," then optimize. A Kelly/ruin objective, not expected-value.
commit early = risk
Because orders are irreversible, an early commitment can foreclose the goal. The agent must reason about option value — keep the plan feasible before spending.
no retry
τ-bench's pass^k retries the whole episode. Here, within an episode there is no undo, so the agent must be right the first time on the mutating steps — the SABER thesis with teeth.

How I'd design it (so it stays a reasoning problem, not arithmetic)

State

  • Required basket R = {milk, saffron, basmati, yogurt, …}, a deadline T (6 PM), a budget B ($50).
  • Vendors V: each offers a subset of R at item prices, with a delivery fee, a tip policy, and a delivery-time window (deterministic → a distribution → adversarial, as a difficulty knob).
  • Consumable agent state: remaining budget, current clock, set of committed (irreversible) orders.

Evaluation — the part that makes it publishable

Solver oracle = no annotation errors

A small solver (ILP / search — the lab already runs Fast Downward) computes per instance: is there a feasible plan? the min-cost feasible plan, the fastest plan. SABER's #1 complaint about τ-bench — broken hand annotations — cannot happen here. Ground truth is computed.

Metrics that capture irreversibility

  • Success: all items ≤ T and total ≤ B (binary, objective — no user-sim judging).
  • Cost-optimality gap: agent spend ÷ solver optimum.
  • Robust success (pass^k analog): success rate over stochastic delivery realizations of the same plan.
  • Ruin rate: fraction of episodes ending in an irreversible foreclosed state (committed budget makes the goal infeasible).

Where it could go wrong — and how to defuse it

PitfallDefuse
It collapses into "call the optimizer." If fully observable & deterministic, the LLM just formulates an ILP and the reasoning vanishes.Keep partial observability (must search, search costs time), add stochastic delivery (tail reasoning), and elicit constraints conversationally. The skill becomes formulating + sequencing + hedging under uncertainty, not solving a handed-over LP.
Difficulty becomes arithmetic. LLMs are weak at adding fees+tip; that's a known crutch (τ-bench ships a calculate tool for it).Give them a calculate tool so arithmetic isn't the bottleneck. Difficulty should live in the combinatorial sourcing + deadline risk, not in summing three numbers.
User-sim reward hacking (the τ-bench disease — see overview).Grade on the objective outcome (delivered on time, under budget), never on a simulated user's "satisfaction." Your design already allows this — lean into it.
Synthetic feels toy.Parameterize a generator (à la PlanBench/IPC) for verifiability, but seed prices/vendors/ETAs from realistic distributions. Verifiability > photorealism for a benchmark.
Irreversibility can make it unsolvable-by-luck. A bad stochastic draw fails even an optimal plan.Separate plan quality (did it choose the max-feasible-probability plan?) from realized outcome. Report both; reward the agent for the decision, not the dice.

My verdict

Build it — it's the right idea for this lab. It targets a real, nameable gap (irreversibility / non-ergodic resources) that τ-bench and even SABER explicitly do not model, and it inherits the lab's strongest asset: solver-verifiable ground truth, which sidesteps the annotation-quality problem SABER spent a whole paper fixing.

The single design discipline that decides whether it's a great benchmark or a toy: make the difficulty live in the coupling — shared budget × conjunctive deadline × irreversible commitment under uncertainty — and keep arithmetic and pure optimization off to the side (a tool, an oracle). Do that, and you have the planning-under-resources counterpart to τ-bench: same conversational tool-use skin, but now the agent has to reason about consequences it cannot take back.

Concrete next step: implement L2 (deterministic biryani) as a generator + ILP oracle + 5 hand-seeds, wire it into the live-agent harness on the explorer page (same API loop, swap the tool backend), and measure cost-gap + ruin-rate on Opus/Sonnet. That's a weekend MVP that already shows the τ-bench → non-ergodic delta.

Naming, if you want one: RuinBench / τ-Resource / DeadlineCart. The "avoid the absorbing state" framing is the hook reviewers will remember.
